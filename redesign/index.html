How a study works

From first idea to first scan, in order. Each step links to the full details.

1. Get ethics approval

  • All research conducted in the DBIC must have approval from Dartmouth’s Committee for the Protection of Human Subjects (CPHS).
  • Everyone working on the study completes IRB education (the CITI Group 2 Social/Behavioral Basic course) and is listed on the CPHS protocol.
CPHS education requirements

2. Apply to the DBIC

  • Send the DBIC application with your CPHS protocol, the CPHS-stamped consent form, and a supplemental description of your study design.
  • New protocols are reviewed before they begin; incomplete submissions wait until they are complete.
  • Courtney Rogers is happy to talk it through by Zoom or in person.
Protocol instructions

3. Get trained

  • Everyone completes MRI safety training: an in-person session with the MRI technologist (up to four people per class) plus an online module.
  • To run the scanner yourself, become an operator-in-training, complete at least 10 hours of hands-on training with a certified operator, and pass a final competency check with the MRI technologist present.
  • At least two people are in the control room at all times: a certified scan operator and a safety-trained assistant.
Safety trainingOperator training

4. Request scan time

  • With an approved protocol, email your request to Courtney Rogers, who schedules all scanner activity.
  • Time is allocated in three tiers (grant-funded, department-funded, then everyone else), each with a monthly request window.
  • Peak hours are weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Evenings and weekends need both a certified operator and a certified scan buddy.
Scheduling detailsScanning rates

5. Scan and analyze

  • Scan time is logged and invoiced monthly for each protocol; the PI or designee reviews each invoice.
  • After each session, confirm that your data transferred to the DBIC server.
  • Analyze on the Discovery cluster, with 14 public datasets already hosted alongside.
Computer resourcesUsing Discovery

Stuck at any step? Email Courtney Rogers, who manages protocols, scheduling and training.

A research scanner, by design

In November 1999 Dartmouth became the first liberal arts college in the country to own and operate an MRI machine strictly for research. That is still the point.

The scanner

3T with a 60 cm bore, 0.25 ppm homogeneity over a 40 cm sphere, and simultaneous multislice. Vendor packages cover BOLD, diffusion and spectroscopy.

Head coils

64-, 32- and 20-channel phased-array coils. The 64-channel coil delivers the highest SNR and parallel-imaging performance.

Safety & compliance

How we keep every scan safe

Working next to a powerful magnet and with human participants comes with firm rules. These are the commitments behind every scan, with the documents behind them.

Human-subjects approval comes firstAll research conducted in the DBIC must have CPHS approval, and all personnel complete IRB education.
Two people in the control room, alwaysAt least one certified scan operator and one safety-trained assistant are present at all times.
Everyone is screenedParticipants and personnel are screened for MRI safety and metal, with metal detectors and a controlled airlock door to the MRI suite.
Trained and certified operatorsSafety training for all research personnel; operator certification requires at least 10 hours of hands-on training.
Emergencies and incidents have a procedureEmergency practices, an incident-reporting policy, and cleaning and housekeeping standards are published and part of every training.
